# 05 · Risks & Challenges to the Product
**Owner:** Anton · Raised, not decided. Temi is the tie-breaker; the pivot stands unless the team says otherwise.

These are written down so a judge cannot raise something we have not already discussed.

### 1. The reward loop is the weakest link in the business model
Points → money means someone is paying cash for engagement. Ayodele has already routed this correctly: a **separate agreed reward pool with a liability cap**, not hidden inside the SaaS fee. Design supports it by naming the funder on screen. The residual risk is real though: if a bank funds £5 credits, our platform fee has to be small next to their reward spend, or the CFO does the arithmetic and stops the pilot. Worth having a number for "reward cost per active user per month" before the pitch.

### 2. "Standalone app" and "bank distribution" pull against each other
The pivot away from an in-app tab is right commercially — a universal API scales and an in-app tab doesn't. But it means we inherit the hardest problem in consumer fintech: getting a young person to download and re-open a second app. Ayodele flags this as the biggest risk and I agree. Design's answer is the sub-40-second loop and the handoff screen. That's a mitigation, not a proof.

### 3. Consumer Duty is supporting rationale, not the sale
The original brief leans on FCA obligation ("banks have a legal obligation and allocated budgets"). Ayodele's research is more careful and more accurate: PRIN 2A.5 supports the case but there is **no evidenced average education budget** and this does not prove compliance. If anyone says "banks are legally required to buy this" on stage, a fintech CEO in the front row will correct us and we lose the room. The Business & Reach wording is the safe version — use it verbatim.

### 4. Two audiences in one pitch
The brief asks for a secondary-school view and a young-professional view; the business work concludes 18–25 is the beachhead. Both are now in the prototype, but the *story* must be: Maya is the business, Jayden is the proof it scales. If we split the narrative evenly we look unfocused.

### 5. The AI claim needs to survive one follow-up question
Right now the intelligence is honest arithmetic plus habit selection from the user's own categories. That is genuinely useful and I would not oversell it. If a judge asks "what's the AI?", the strong answer is the habit generation and personalisation — so it is worth Priya wiring **one real model call** that turns the spending breakdown into three habit suggestions, rather than us picking from a fixed list. Small change, removes the only soft spot in the demo.

### 6. What I'd cut if we slip
The new-goal screen. It is the least surprising thing we show, and the timeline reads fine if the goal already exists. Everything else earns its stage time.
